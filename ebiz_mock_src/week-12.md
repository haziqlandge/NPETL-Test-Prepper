Q1) What is the central aim of auction design, as described in the economic considerations lecture?
A) To keep bidders' private valuations hidden from every party
B) To guarantee that every auction ends exactly at the seller's reserve price
C) To design auction rules with desirable properties that satisfy the auctioneer's needs

C) Interdependent value model

Q8) Under which set of assumptions do all basic auction formats generate the same expected revenue?

Q11) With independent private values and values uniform between 50 and 100, how does expected revenue change as the number of bidders increases?

Q12) Which behaviour is typical of a risk-averse bidder who worries about not winning?

B) Ignores price signals entirely

Q13) When bidders' valuations are interdependent, which statement holds?
A) English and second-price auctions remain revenue equivalent under all conditions
B) The first-price auction always yields more revenue than the English auction
C) The English auction likely yields more revenue than both first-price and second-price

Q14) A firm wins an auction for offshore drilling rights in a common-value setting after bidding its own estimate, and then finds that every rival's estimate was lower. Which concept explains this outcome, and what correction should bidders apply?

Q15) Modelling an auction as a Bayesian game means what?
